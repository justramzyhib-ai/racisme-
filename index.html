What is racism?

Racism is judging, excluding or treating someone badly because of their skin colour, ethnic background, culture or religion. It hurts people and divides communities.

Our differences make us interesting. Our respect makes us stronger.
Notice it

Racism can happen in simple moments.

It can happen at school, online or in daily life. Knowing the signs helps us choose a better response.

01

Hurtful jokes

Making fun of someone’s name, accent, religion or origin is not harmless. It can make a person feel unsafe.

02

Leaving people out

Ignoring someone or refusing to include them because they look or speak differently is unfair.

03

Hate online

Racist messages, comments or videos online should not be shared. Report them and ask for help.

Act with courage

Four simple actions can make a real difference.

We do not need to be perfect. We can start with small choices that make people feel seen, safe and respected.

Learn and listen

Discover other cultures and listen to people’s experiences with an open mind.

Speak up

Do not laugh at racist jokes or stay silent when someone is treated unfairly. Say, “That is not okay.”

Include everyone

Be kind, make new friends and make sure nobody feels left out at school, online or in your community.

Report and support

Tell a trusted adult, teacher or school leader about racism. Support the person who has been hurt.
